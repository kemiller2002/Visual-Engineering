## Highest Confidence Areas

- Visual perception is spatially nonuniform and fixation-relative.
- Peripheral identification is constrained by crowding.
- Perceptual grouping determines the units on which later cognition operates.
- Attention reflects competition among salience, goals, history, and learned value.
- Scene and object information interact bidirectionally.
- Natural vision depends on repeated active sampling through eye, head, and body movement.

## Lowest Confidence Areas

- Whether predictive coding is the correct neural implementation rather than one useful
  computational description.
- Whether prediction error can be measured as a practical composition metric.
- The boundary between unconscious contextual processing and conscious integration.
- The amount of scene understanding available from the first feedforward sweep.
- How to predict fixation sequences from composition without task-specific data.

## Largest Remaining Unknown

> **Can Composition Science predict how a viewer will sample, interpret, and revise a
> scene over time well enough to forecast comprehension and action?**

- Active vision and fixation sequencing

- Perceptual separation and crowding

- Perception is a strictly linear pipeline.
- Meaning appears only after complete object recognition.
- Context always facilitates perception.
- Expected stimuli always evoke reduced neural activity.
- Good composition can be defined simply as minimizing prediction error.

# Executive Summary

## Original Question

How does raw sensory input become an actionable understanding of a visual scene, and can
this process provide a predictive foundation for Composition Science?

## What Was Accomplished

Five research cycles examined:

1. Rapid scene and object processing.
2. Recurrent processing and visual awareness.
3. Predictive processing and expectation effects.
4. Active vision and fixation-driven evidence acquisition.
5. Translation into a composition theory.

The research compared feedforward, recurrent, predictive-processing, and active-
perception accounts. It explicitly searched for evidence contradicting the strongest
predictive-processing claims.

## Major Discoveries

### 1. Scene construction begins rapidly but does not finish in one pass

Humans can extract broad scene and object information from brief exposures. Current
evidence supports partly parallel early processing of scene structure and object
content rather than a strict sequence in which scenes must be recognized before
objects or objects before scenes.

### 2. Recurrent processing is functionally important

Masking studies support the view that an initial feedforward sweep can occur without
full conscious access, while recurrent or reentrant processing contributes to stable,
reportable perception and integration.

### 3. Context is bidirectional and conditional

Scene context can facilitate object recognition when signals are weak or ambiguous.
The same expectations can impair detection of unexpected details or alter decisions
when sensory evidence is strong. Context should therefore be modeled as a prior that
changes inference, not as an unconditional benefit.

### 4. Predictive coding remains underdetermined

Expectation effects are real, but neural expectation suppression is inconsistent
after controlling for repetition, adaptation, novelty, attention, and surprise.
Prediction is strongly supported as a functional property of perception; a particular
prediction-error neural code is not yet established as the unique explanation.

### 5. Natural perception is active

Viewers move their eyes several times per second and may also move their head and body.
These actions change the sensory input and select the next evidence sample. Composition
therefore influences not only what is represented, but what viewers choose to inspect.

## Core Theory Update

The earlier linear chain:

```text
Features → Groups → Objects → Hierarchy → Mental Model → Decision → Action
```

is replaced by a recurrent loop:

```text
Prior state and goals
        ↓
Rapid parallel scene/object hypothesis
        ↓
Perceptual grouping and provisional hierarchy
        ↓
Uncertainty and task relevance
        ↓
Attention and action selection
        ↓
Eye/head/body movement
        ↓
New high-resolution evidence
        ↓
Recurrent integration and model revision
        ↺
```

## Overall Confidence

**Medium-high**

The hybrid recurrent active-perception model is strongly supported at a broad level.
The exact neural implementation and practical mathematical parameters remain uncertain.

# Repository Context

This package extends previous project work that established:

- Human-scale effects are multidimensional and context-sensitive.
- Visual hierarchy is an alignment problem among perceptual, behavioral, and task priorities.
- Effective composition externalizes cognitive structure.
- Gestalt grouping defines perceptual units.
- Grouping compresses information but can hide within-group differences.

The missing layer was temporal: how a viewer constructs and revises an understanding

# Current Understanding

## Provisional Core Statement

> **Composition structures the evidence available to an active perceiver, influencing
> the hypotheses formed, the uncertainty remaining, the samples selected next, and the
> actions considered possible.**

This is stronger than defining composition as arrangement, but more defensible than
claiming that composition merely minimizes prediction error.

## Distinctions Required

### Prediction

A representation of likely sensory, semantic, or action-related states.

### Expectation

A learned or instructed belief about what is likely.

### Prior

A probability distribution influencing interpretation before current evidence is fully
evaluated.

### Prediction Error

A mismatch between predicted and observed input. Its exact neural implementation is
theory-dependent.

### Recurrent Processing

Processing in which later or higher-level activity feeds back to earlier or lower-level
areas, or in which information circulates through networks rather than moving once in
a single direction.

### Active Perception

Perception that includes actions chosen to obtain better sensory evidence.

### Scene Gist

A rapidly available coarse representation of scene category, layout, or overall meaning.

### Verification

Additional sampling or processing used to confirm, revise, or reject an initial hypothesis.

# Key Discoveries

## KD-001: Initial Scene Understanding Is Parallel and Provisional

The strongest synthesis supports partly parallel computation of scene and object
information. An initial scene gist does not require complete serial identification of
every object. Objects can also influence scene interpretation.

**Evidence:** EV-COMP-051, EV-COMP-052  
**Affected hypotheses:** HY-COMP-033, HY-COMP-037  
**Confidence:** High

## KD-002: Rapid Recognition Does Not Imply Complete Understanding

Brief exposure can support category judgments, but normal scene processing across an
eye fixation requires more time and supports richer object identity, relationships,
memory, and action planning.

**Evidence:** EV-COMP-052, EV-COMP-053  
**Affected hypotheses:** HY-COMP-037  
**Confidence:** High

## KD-003: Recurrent Processing Supports Stable Conscious Perception

Backward masking can preserve early feedforward activity while disrupting later
reentrant processing and awareness. This supports a two-stage distinction without
proving one universal consciousness theory.

**Evidence:** EV-COMP-054  
**Affected hypotheses:** HY-COMP-033, HY-COMP-038  
**Confidence:** Medium-high

## KD-004: Contextual Predictions Can Help or Hurt

Congruent context can sharpen or facilitate perception under weak sensory evidence.
Incongruent objects can receive processing advantages when the input is clear because
they produce novelty or conflict. Composition should not maximize predictability
indiscriminately.

**Evidence:** EV-COMP-051, EV-COMP-055  
**Affected hypotheses:** HY-COMP-034, HY-COMP-036  
**Confidence:** High

## KD-005: Expectation Suppression Is Not a General Proof of Predictive Coding

Many neural suppression findings are confounded by repetition, adaptation, attention,
novelty, and surprise. Genuine expectation suppression appears reliable in a narrower
subset of paradigms than commonly claimed.

**Evidence:** EV-COMP-056, EV-COMP-057  
**Affected hypotheses:** HY-COMP-035, HY-COMP-036  
**Confidence:** High

## KD-006: High-Level Unconscious Integration Remains Unresolved

Some studies suggest object-context integration without awareness. Replication failures
and null neural results prevent treating this as established. High-level integration
may require awareness, or current methods may be insufficiently sensitive.

**Evidence:** EV-COMP-058  
**Affected hypotheses:** HY-COMP-034, HY-COMP-038  
**Confidence:** Medium-low

## KD-007: Eye Movements Are Part of the Computation

Attention and eye-movement preparation interact. Saccades bring selected information
into the fovea, and perception integrates information across samples. The viewer is not
a stationary sensor.

**Evidence:** EV-COMP-053, EV-COMP-059, EV-COMP-060  
**Affected hypotheses:** HY-COMP-039  
**Confidence:** High

## KD-008: Composition Guides Evidence Acquisition

Landmarks, hierarchy, semantic expectations, and visible actions influence where a
viewer looks next. Composition therefore changes the sequence and cost of model
revision.

**Evidence:** EV-COMP-051, EV-COMP-059, prior EV records from wayfinding research  
**Affected hypotheses:** HY-COMP-039, TH-COMP-004  
**Confidence:** Medium-high

# Hypothesis Registry

## HY-COMP-033: Iterative Perception Hypothesis

### Original Statement

Perception is iterative rather than sequential.

### Evidence For

- Rapid feedforward processing followed by recurrent integration.
- Bidirectional object-scene interactions.
- Repeated fixation-driven sampling.

### Evidence Against

- Some scene-category information is available extremely rapidly and may be supported
  by a predominantly feedforward sweep.
- Not every perceptual judgment requires long recurrent processing.

### Revision

**Perception combines fast feedforward inference with recurrent and active refinement;
it is neither purely serial nor purely recurrent.**

### Status

Supported after revision.

### Confidence

High.

---

## HY-COMP-034: Preconscious Prediction Hypothesis

### Original Statement

Visual composition influences prediction before conscious reasoning begins.

### Evidence For

- Context and learned regularities affect early visual representations.
- Scene information can bias object processing rapidly.
- Attention and saccade preparation modulate early visual responses.

### Evidence Against

- High-level object-scene integration without awareness is not reliably established.
- Some effects may reflect decision, attention, or memory rather than altered perception.

### Revision

**Composition can rapidly bias perceptual inference before deliberate reasoning, but the
level of semantic integration available without awareness remains uncertain.**

### Status

Provisionally supported.

### Confidence

Medium.

---

## HY-COMP-035: Uncertainty-Minimization Hypothesis

### Original Statement

Perception minimizes uncertainty rather than maximizing information.

### Evidence For

- Priors reduce ambiguity.
- Active sampling appears directed toward task-relevant uncertainty.
- Contextual regularities speed search and recognition in some conditions.

### Evidence Against

- Novel or incongruent information often attracts attention.
- Exploration may deliberately seek information with high expected gain.
- “Uncertainty minimization” can become unfalsifiably broad unless task and time scale
  are specified.

### Revision

**Perception allocates sampling to reduce task-relevant uncertainty under constraints,
while also detecting unexpected evidence that may require model revision.**

### Status

Supported only in revised, bounded form.

### Confidence

Medium.

---

## HY-COMP-036: Prediction-Error Composition Hypothesis

### Original Statement

Good composition reduces prediction error.

### Evidence For

- Stable conventions and coherent grouping reduce interpretation cost.
- Valid context can improve recognition under uncertainty.
- Consistent local cues support mental models.

### Evidence Against

- Unexpected elements can be detected or processed better.
- Excessively predictable composition can hide anomalies.
- Prediction-error neural signatures are not uniquely established.
- High-stakes systems sometimes need deliberate prediction error to expose warnings.

### Revision

**Good composition manages prediction error: it reduces unnecessary error while
preserving or amplifying task-relevant violations.**

### Status

Original rejected; revised hypothesis retained.

### Confidence

Medium-high.

---

## HY-COMP-037: Rapid Gist Hypothesis

### Statement

A coarse scene-and-object hypothesis is formed before detailed serial inspection.

### Evidence For

- Rapid scene categorization.
- Parallel scene and object processing.
- Global features and object constellations support early interpretation.

### Evidence Against

- Gist does not guarantee correct object identity, relation understanding, or action.
- Low-level image statistics can contribute to some rapid judgments.

### Status

Supported.

### Confidence

High.

---

## HY-COMP-038: Recurrent Integration Hypothesis

### Statement

Stable conscious scene understanding depends substantially on recurrent processing.

### Evidence For

- Masking selectively disrupts later processing and visibility.
- Contextual relation processing recruits distributed and frontal systems.
- Rich scene processing takes longer than initial gist extraction.

### Evidence Against

- The exact boundary between feedforward and recurrent contribution varies by task.
- Conscious report and integration may not be identical mechanisms.

### Status

Provisionally supported.

### Confidence

Medium-high.

---

## HY-COMP-039: Active Sampling Hypothesis

### Statement

Viewers construct scene understanding by selecting successive evidence samples through
eye, head, and body movements.

### Evidence For

- Saccades several times per second.
- Attention and movement preparation interact.
- Natural search uses viewpoint selection.
- Peripheral limits make repeated foveation necessary.

### Evidence Against

- Some global scene judgments require only one brief exposure.
- Covert attention can select information without an eye movement.

### Status

Supported.

### Confidence

High.

# Failed Assumptions

## FA-COMP-014: Linear Perception Pipeline

### Assumption

Visual processing proceeds through a single one-way chain.

### Why It Failed

Object and scene processing are partly parallel, higher-level context feeds back, and
active sampling repeatedly changes the input.

### Consequence

All project diagrams must show recurrence and action loops.

---

## FA-COMP-015: Context Always Facilitates

### Assumption

Expected objects are always recognized more accurately and rapidly.

### Why It Failed

Context effects depend on sensory uncertainty, task, novelty, and the distinction
between perception and decision.

### Consequence

Replace “consistency is always easier” with a conditional law.

---

## FA-COMP-016: Expectation Suppression Proves Predictive Coding

### Assumption

Lower response to expected stimuli is direct evidence of prediction-error coding.

### Why It Failed

Repetition, adaptation, attention, novelty, and surprise provide competing explanations.

### Consequence

Do not use neural suppression alone as evidence for TH-COMP-005.

---

## FA-COMP-017: Prediction Error Is Always Bad

### Assumption

The best composition minimizes all surprise.

### Why It Failed

Warnings, anomalies, errors, novelty, and change require detectable violations.

### Consequence

Composition must distinguish unnecessary mismatch from useful mismatch.

---

## FA-COMP-018: One Glance Equals Full Understanding

### Assumption

Rapid gist extraction provides an actionable complete scene representation.

### Why It Failed

Detailed recognition, relational understanding, verification, and action planning
require further processing and sampling.

### Consequence

Measure first-glance comprehension separately from task completion.

---

## FA-COMP-019: Static Layout Is the Whole Stimulus

### Assumption

A screenshot contains enough information to model perception.

### Why It Failed

Fixations, scrolling, motion, interaction, viewpoint, and transitions change the
available evidence.

### Consequence

Composition analysis must include temporal and interactive structure.

# Proposed Theory

## TH-COMP-005: Active Inference Composition Theory

### Statement

> **A composition is an evidence environment for an active perceiver. It shapes the
> initial hypotheses formed from a glance, the uncertainty that remains, the locations
> sampled next, the relationships verified, and the actions made available.**

### Mechanism

1. Existing goals, expectations, and learned conventions establish priors.
2. Coarse visual features support rapid, partly parallel scene and object hypotheses.
3. Grouping and figure-ground processes construct provisional units.
4. Salience, task relevance, and uncertainty establish priorities.
5. Attention and action systems select the next sample.
6. Eye, head, body, or interface actions change the sensory evidence.
7. Recurrent processing integrates new evidence with the current model.
8. The model stabilizes sufficiently for action or remains open for further sampling.
9. Unexpected evidence can revise the model or trigger a new search strategy.

### Assumptions

- Perception is resource-constrained.
- The retina and visual field are nonuniform.
- Goals influence sampling.
- Priors can help or bias.
- Composition changes evidence accessibility.
- Action is part of perception in natural tasks.
- Conscious report is not a complete proxy for all perceptual processing.

### Boundary Conditions

- Some rapid judgments can occur after a single brief exposure.
- Not all prediction effects alter phenomenology.
- Not all recurrent processing is conscious.
- The theory does not require one specific predictive-coding neural implementation.
- The theory predicts task performance, not aesthetic preference by itself.

### Confidence

Medium-high.

# Candidate Laws

## LAW-COMP-033: Feedforward-Recurrent Complementarity Law

### Hypothesis

Rapid feedforward processing produces an initial scene hypothesis, while recurrent
processing refines, integrates, and stabilizes it.

### Prediction

Brief or masked exposure will preserve coarse category and hierarchy judgments better
than relational, contextual, or state-specific judgments.

### Supporting Evidence

- EV-COMP-052
- EV-COMP-054

### Counter Evidence

Some rapid judgments may include more semantic information than a simple coarse-to-fine
description predicts.

### Confidence

Medium-high.

## LAW-COMP-034: Conditional Context Law

### Hypothesis

Context improves perception when it constrains ambiguous evidence, but can impair
detection or identification when expectations suppress, misdirect, or compete with
clear contradictory evidence.

### Prediction

Context congruency benefits will be largest under low signal reliability and may reverse
for clear unexpected targets.

### Supporting Evidence

- EV-COMP-051
- EV-COMP-055

### Counter Evidence

Different tasks may show facilitation at decision rather than perceptual stages.

### Confidence

High.

---

## LAW-COMP-035: Task-Relevant Uncertainty Law

### Hypothesis

Visual sampling is allocated to reduce uncertainty that matters for the current task,
not uncertainty in general.

### Prediction

The same composition will produce different fixation sequences under different tasks
even when the visual stimulus is unchanged.

### Supporting Evidence

- EV-COMP-059
- EV-COMP-060

### Counter Evidence

Strong salience and learned history can capture attention despite task irrelevance.

### Confidence

High.

---

## LAW-COMP-036: Controlled Violation Law

### Hypothesis

Effective composition makes ordinary structure predictable while making consequential
violations easy to detect.

### Prediction

A warning that violates a stable visual grammar will be found faster than a warning
styled as an ordinary member of the system, provided the violation remains interpretable.

### Supporting Evidence

- EV-COMP-055
- EV-COMP-056
- Prior salience and landmark evidence

### Counter Evidence

Excessive novelty can distract, confuse, or produce habituation.

### Confidence

Medium-high.

---

## LAW-COMP-037: Fixation-Dependent Evidence Law

### Hypothesis

The information available for scene interpretation changes with each fixation because
foveal and peripheral processing differ.

### Prediction

Layouts that require peripheral identification of fine or crowded details will produce
additional saccades, longer search, and greater error.

### Supporting Evidence

- EV-COMP-053
- EV-COMP-059
- Prior crowding evidence

### Counter Evidence

Covert attention and learned context can partially compensate.

### Confidence

High.

---

## LAW-COMP-038: Evidence Continuity Law

### Hypothesis

Stable visual correspondences across eye movements and interface state changes reduce
the cost of updating the scene model.

### Prediction

Transitions preserving spatial and object continuity will reduce reorientation time
relative to abrupt replacements with equal final content.

### Supporting Evidence

- EV-COMP-059
- Visual stability literature

### Counter Evidence

Direct composition-specific experiments remain missing.

### Confidence

Medium.

---

## LAW-COMP-039: Verification Cost Law

### Hypothesis

The more costly an error, the more composition should support deliberate verification
rather than relying on context-driven gist.

### Prediction

High-stakes interfaces with visible state, confirmation evidence, and consequence
preview will reduce expectation-based errors.

### Supporting Evidence

- Context can bias perception.
- Gist is incomplete.
- Externalized memory reduces internal reconstruction.

### Counter Evidence

Excessive verification can create delay, fatigue, and confirmation habituation.

### Confidence

Medium-high.

# Decision Framework

## DF-COMP-002: Perceptual Evidence Path Review

Use this framework to analyze a composition.

### Step 1: Define the Task

What must the observer detect, identify, compare, understand, decide, or do?

### Step 2: Define the Initial View

What is visible before any eye movement, scroll, interaction, or locomotion?

### Step 3: Predict the Initial Hypothesis

What scene category, hierarchy, and action possibilities are likely to be inferred?

### Step 4: Identify Ambiguities

Which elements remain uncertain, crowded, hidden, semantically weak, or expectation-dependent?

### Step 5: Predict the Next Sample

Where is the observer likely to look or move next, and why?

### Step 6: Check Evidence Continuity

Does the next view preserve enough spatial, semantic, and object continuity to update
the model without rebuilding it?

### Step 7: Check Violations

Are anomalies and consequential state changes strong enough to break expectation?

### Step 8: Check Verification

Can the observer confirm identity, state, consequence, and completion when the cost of
error is high?

### Step 9: Measure

Record:

- first-fixation target
- time to first fixation
- fixation count
- scanpath length
- recognition accuracy
- relational accuracy
- state comprehension
- task completion time
- error and recovery rate
- confidence calibration

### Step 10: Falsify

Create an alternative composition with the same content but a different evidence path.
Test whether the predicted advantage appears.

## RISK-COMP-021: Predictive-Processing Overreach

# Research Journal

## JR-COMP-005-01 — Competing Processing Architectures

### Objective

Compare serial bottom-up, predictive-first, recurrent, and interactive models.

### Hypothesis

A purely serial hierarchy is insufficient.

### Evidence Found

Scene and object processing are partly parallel and bidirectional.

### Evidence Against

Some rapid categorization can be supported by a largely feedforward sweep.

### Analysis

The correct model is hybrid: feedforward initiation plus recurrent refinement.

### Conclusion

Reject a strict serial pipeline.

### Confidence

High.

### Next Step

Test the role of recurrence in awareness and integration.

---

## JR-COMP-005-02 — Recurrent Processing

### Objective

Determine whether recurrent activity is necessary for stable scene understanding.

### Hypothesis

Masking disrupts recurrent processing more than initial feedforward activity.

### Evidence Found

Electrophysiological masking evidence supports this distinction.

### Evidence Against

The precise relationship among recurrence, report, attention, and consciousness remains debated.

### Analysis

Recurrent processing is important, but should not be equated automatically with consciousness.

### Conclusion

Retain recurrent integration with bounded claims.

### Confidence

Medium-high.

### Next Step

Test predictive-processing claims and confounds.

---

## JR-COMP-005-03 — Predictive Coding Falsification

### Objective

Test whether expectation suppression provides robust evidence for predictive coding.

### Hypothesis

Expected stimuli should reliably evoke lower neural responses independent of repetition.

### Evidence Found

Some long-term statistical-learning studies support genuine expectation suppression.

### Evidence Against

Across many paradigms, effects are inconsistent or confounded by adaptation, repetition,
attention, novelty, and surprise.

### Analysis

Prediction is functionally important, but neural suppression is not a general proof.

### Conclusion

Reject the strong expectation-suppression hypothesis.

### Confidence

High.

### Next Step

Examine when context helps or hurts.

---

## JR-COMP-005-04 — Contextual Facilitation and Impairment

### Objective

Determine whether predictable context always improves perception.

### Hypothesis

Congruent objects should always be processed better.

### Evidence Found

Congruency helps under ambiguity and weak signals.

### Evidence Against

Incongruent objects can receive an advantage under clear sensory input.

### Analysis

Context changes priors and allocation, producing conditional rather than uniformly
beneficial effects.

### Conclusion

Replace facilitation with Conditional Context Law.

### Confidence

High.

### Next Step

Examine active sampling.

---

## JR-COMP-005-05 — Active Perception

### Objective

Determine whether eye movements should be modeled as outputs or as part of perception.

### Hypothesis

Natural scene understanding requires active evidence acquisition.

### Evidence Found

Attention, saccade planning, visual stability, and fixation-dependent encoding interact.

### Evidence Against

Some coarse judgments occur without an eye movement.

### Analysis

Active sampling is not required for every judgment, but it is fundamental to rich natural
scene understanding.

### Conclusion

Promote active perception to the core theory.

### Confidence

High.

### Next Step

Research fixation guidance and visual search.
