# EX-ITTEN-005 — Warm/cool cross-cultural transfer

## Question and falsifier

Are hue–temperature judgments stable across language and climate, and do they predict
affect, urgency, or distance? The universal account fails with substantial
group-by-hue interactions or dissociation among thermal, affective, urgency, and depth
outcomes.

## Work completed

A fixed-seed toy power simulation examined a standardized cross-group interaction of
`.25`, with 2,000 runs at 30, 60, 120, and 240 analyzable participants per stratum.

## Pilot results

Estimated power was `.081`, `.205`, `.4915`, and `.861`, respectively. The provisional
planning value is therefore 240 analyzable participants per language/climate stratum,
not 120. This is only a rough planning simulation; pilot variance and the final
mixed-effects structure must replace it.

## Field experiment ready to run

- Sampling: at least four language/climate strata selected prospectively; record
  multilingualism, residence history, climate exposure, color vocabulary, education,
  and display/device characteristics.
- Stimuli: calibrated hue circle at crossed lightness/chroma/intensity levels; include
  achromatic controls and natural/material scenes.
- Separate outcomes: thermal rating, expected physical temperature, affect, arousal,
  urgency, nearness, preference, and free naming. Never collapse them into “warmth.”
- Translation: forward translation, independent back-translation, cognitive interview,
  and measurement-invariance testing.
- Design: randomized within-person stimulus trials; avoid red/blue response labels and
  culturally loaded icons.
- Analysis: hierarchical ordinal models; group × hue × intensity interactions;
  invariance and leave-one-culture-out prediction.

## Current position / continuation

**Stage:** power scaffold complete; zero survey or participant responses collected.
**Next action:** secure cultural research partners, translate instruments, conduct
cognitive interviews, then estimate variance from a 20-person-per-stratum pilot.
